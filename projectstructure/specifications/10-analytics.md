# 10 — Analytics rules and wording

Analytics is a feed, without the Library group column. Each tab has independent filters, scroll, and a stable selection seed until a fresh open/explicit refresh. All cards share one snapshot revision; on updates refresh coherently and preserve scroll. Every metric exposes population/denominator and a way to inspect contributing entries.

## Rating distribution and rarity

Population: active experienced entries with a current overall rating. Planned, dropped, Unrated, and trash are excluded. With fewer than 20 rated works, render no analytics feed and invite the user to explore and rate more works. At 20 or more, show the feed. The first histogram shows only integer scores with at least one work, and exposes both the count and share of all rated works for each shown score. This puts rating rarity next to the distribution it describes; there is no second rarity section. Show the sample size, mean (one decimal), median, population standard deviation, and tied modes where useful.

Use a rounded, readable count axis with evenly spaced ticks and enough headroom above the tallest bar for its count label. Keep the count above every bar, including the tallest. Use theme-aware chart colors that remain distinct in dark, dusk, light, and reading themes. A plotted percentage is `100 × count(score) / N`; it is separate from overall-rank Top percentage. Percentages may sum to 99.9/100.1 after rounding.

For N ≥ 20, show at most two compatible, localized observations supported by deterministic shape rules. Below that threshold, suppress distributions, comparisons, top lists, and boundary review together. Avoid a partially populated empty page.

1. **Concentrated score:** one bin contains ≥60%. Explain that many recorded works share the peak; this can reflect a narrow scale, similar recorded experiences, or a first-pass selection of works.
2. **Separated peaks:** two local peaks each contain ≥20%, are at least three scores apart, and every intermediate bin is lower than both. Name both peaks, including ties, and describe the gap as potentially reflecting different kinds of recorded experience or expectations.
3. **Near-flat:** at least eight occupied bins and coefficient of variation of all ten bin counts ≤0.35. Describe the range as broadly used without claiming a personality trait.
4. **Broad spread:** standard deviation ≥2.2 and at least six occupied bins. Explain that low and high experiences are represented, which may reflect a wider range of recorded works or different expectations.
5. **Dominant band:** total share of 1–4, 5–6, 7–8, or 9–10 is ≥45%, exceeds each other band's share by ≥10 percentage points, and contains a modal bin. For 1–4, suggest strict scoring or disappointing recorded works; 5–6, broad sampling or a demanding bar; 7–8, selective choices or leaving less appealing experiences unrecorded; 9–10, favorites or promising choices recorded first. Explain the observed shape before offering a possibility.
6. Otherwise say that several ranges appear without enough evidence for one specific reading.

Keep observations short, general, and tentative. They describe the library snapshot, not the person. Never infer that the user consumes a medium selectively just from an average, call a chart “normal/bell-shaped,” or present one save's exact shape as a universal interpretation.

## Across media types

Plot counts at each score for media types with ≥5 rated works and mark samples below 20 as small. The score axis uses the union of scores with at least one work among those plotted types, so an empty score does not take up a position; hiding a legend entry does not change that axis. Use the saved media type name (localized only when it is a built-in default type); tags such as “Anime” do not rename the “Animation” media type. Type-less entries may appear as “No type,” but they do not support medium-specific comparisons.

Show each plotted type's sample size and its average, median, and share rated 8–10. Separately call out the largest 8–10 share among types with at least 20 rated works; preserve all exact ties and omit the callout if all eligible shares are equal. Do not let small categories lead a comparison.

Interpret pairs only when both samples have ≥20 works. Order comparisons by represented sample size and show each pair's values, peak scores, and explanation together in one compact block that explicitly names both types. Avoid disconnected notes such as “Their most common scores…” whose referent is unclear. Include at most three pairs. If sample counts differ by more than 4×, report the imbalance and omit a directional interpretation.

Use “similar recorded distributions” only when the means differ by <0.35, 8–10 shares by <15 percentage points, and medians by <1. Call a higher average only when the mean gap is ≥0.6 and the median moves in the same direction by at least 0.5. A mean gap ≥0.6 without that matching median is mixed; smaller differences should say that averages are close and note a materially different 8–10 share where relevant. Different or tied modal scores are useful context but do not by themselves establish a preference.

Explanations may mention different expectations, recording choices, or how selectively works are chosen. A book/time-investment example remains a possibility, never an assertion about the user. No significance, causality, preference probability, or personality labels without a separate validated method.

## Ranked media within each tier

Place a ranked line chart after the count-by-score media chart and before top lists. It answers which media types tend to sit nearer the top of each rating tier, using only the canonical explicitly placed order. For every rated tier, derive each type's mean 1-based within-tier rank (rank 1 is highest). If a tier contains N placed works and a type's mean rank is r, compute its raw strength as `(N + 1 - r) / N`; normalize raw strengths across included media types so their displayed weights sum to 1 for that tier. This measures relative position, not how many works of a type exist. The y axis runs from 0 to 1; a higher point means a stronger relative position in that tier.

Show a tier comparison only when it has at least two placed works and at least two media types with placed works before support filtering. Type-less works contribute to ranked coverage and the within-tier order but receive no media-type share. For a type, show zero and explain the exclusion only when both conditions hold: it has fewer than three placed works in that tier, and its count is no more than one quarter of the largest type's count there. A small category with a reasonably balanced representation remains included; a comparatively tiny type with at least three works remains included. If only one type remains eligible after this rule, it may receive 100% of the included media-type strength, with the excluded type shown at zero.

Keep each rated score on the x axis, including a visible gap when that tier cannot support a comparison. Do not allocate axis space to scores with no rated works. Plot a type at a tier only when the tier is comparable, the type has a placed work there, and its weight is known. A sparse-but-placed type excluded by the support rule still has a valid zero point; an unplaced-only type has no point. Break a line at an occupied tier where that type has no valid point. Build the legend from types with at least one plotted point; do not show “No type” just because a type-less group exists elsewhere in the library.

Report rated-work count, placed count, and coverage for each occupied tier. Show exact full coverage as 100%; floor partial percentages to one decimal and cap them at 99.9%, so an incomplete tier never appears complete through rounding. Unplaced works never receive an ordinal rank: a tier can show partial coverage when some works are ranked, while a tier without enough ranked variety is left as a gap. Do not fabricate a point, 100%, or a type preference when no valid ranked comparison exists. Ignore stale, duplicate, unrated, or wrong-tier placement IDs by using the canonical rank index. Omit the whole chart when no tier is available; retain gaps where an occupied tier cannot be compared. Clicking any plotted type point opens the shared comparison table for that score tier, so all type points at that score show the same tier summary. Keep the method explanation under the chart only, not repeated in the modal. Keep the wording tentative and describe within-tier ordering rather than general media preference.

For chart colors, light mode uses the same analytics graph palette as dark mode. Reading mode keeps a distinct media-series palette and uses a sage-to-teal gradient for the first rating histogram.

## Meaningful top lists

Candidate filters: one media type; one established tag; a release decade; type + decade. Do not generate arbitrary predicate combinations. Candidate must have ≥8 placed eligible works for Top 5, ≥3 distinct overall scores OR ≥8 explicitly ordered same-score works, and enough known fields for its predicate. Rank-based lists include placed works only; unplaced works still count in rating distributions but do not have rank, percentile, or Top percentage. Explicit order means `rankingPlaced=true`, through a recorded manual placement or confirmed binary insertion; default append alone does not prove intentional ordering. Analytics reads the same canonical placed order as Ranking and Library at one snapshot revision. Maximum three cards, with different entry sets where possible; near-duplicate sets with Jaccard overlap >0.8 are deduplicated.

Choose a seeded shuffle of eligible candidates on a fresh tab open or Refresh selections, avoid the previous filter when other eligible filters exist, and persist it within the tab. Sort results using canonical order only. A tag remains a valid filter when its works overlap or exactly match a media type's works; retain its own label so users can discover meaningful tags such as Manga or Web Novel. If no candidate qualifies, omit niche cards and show general library stats; do not invent “Top 5 Animation” from two entries.

Year-based lists use only known release years and clearly say “Among works with release years,” plus missing-year count. Missing dates never become year zero/current year. If no eligible dated subset exists, show a request to add dates rather than a year-based result.

## Rating-boundary review

Find adjacent numeric groups k and k−1 that both contain placed entries. Compare lowest-ranked in k with highest-ranked in k−1. If a group has rated but unplaced entries and no placed entry, do not invent a rank-boundary comparison for it; direct the user to place works first. Prioritize 10/9, 9/8, 8/7, then remaining descending boundaries. Do not bridge empty groups (10/8 is not a 10/9 boundary). This review uses full-library boundaries; temporarily filtered Analytics shows that scope explicitly and does not mislabel filtered extremes as actual boundaries.

Actions: Keep both ratings; move upper work down (insert at top of lower group); move lower work up (insert at bottom of upper group); edit either directly. Preview resulting ratings/order. Changing groups writes normal rating history, invalidates boundary cards and recomputes ranks. Keep records the current pair/fingerprint in preferences and portable archives; suppress it until a boundary participant, rating, or relative boundary position changes, or explicit review reset. It must not repeat each launch unchanged. Continue to the next meaningful boundary; end with a concise completion state.

## Acceptance

Fixtures cover empty/singleton, the 19/20 page-visibility threshold, rounded axes and count-label headroom, occupied-score axes with no empty bins, single-score centering, per-score count/percentage labels, one dominant bin, equal and separated peaks, flat/broad shapes, tiny categories, comparisons with close and materially different means, tied high-share leaders, imbalanced type samples, normalized ranked tier weights, conjunction-based small-category exclusion, partial rank coverage, malformed placement references, overlapping type/tag candidates, same-score refresh alternatives, missing dates, and all scores identical. Browser review covers dark, light, reading, and narrow layouts; absent, partial (including an unplaced-only type), and full ranked coverage; the 0–1 weight scale and gaps across missing points; the small-category zero point; incomplete coverage labels; legend membership limited to plotted series; identical whole-tier modal content when different media points in one tier are clicked; method copy only below the chart; modal side padding; and ranked-tier section order. Every statement has a deterministic evidence rule and localized whole-sentence template. No card suggests causal preference from absent data.
